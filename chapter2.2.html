Chapter 2.2

Waterfall: Benefits & Disadvantages

US-5 Traditional Project Management

Understanding when Waterfall works best — and when its rigidity becomes a risk.

Why This Matters for Scrum Masters

Even in Agile environments, understanding Waterfall helps you evaluate project contexts, communicate with stakeholders who come from traditional backgrounds, and make informed decisions about when hybrid approaches might be valuable.

WATERFALL
Structure & Predictability Flexibility
Benefits
  • Clear Structure & Process Control
  • Strong Documentation
  • Easy to Measure Progress
  • Suitable for Stable Projects & Regulated Environments
Disadvantages
  • Limited Flexibility
  • Late Customer Feedback
  • Higher Risk of Requirement Errors
  • Not Ideal for Complex Projects

Waterfall provides structure and predictability but lacks flexibility. This comparison helps project managers quickly evaluate whether the framework fits their project context.

Figure 1: Waterfall Benefits vs Disadvantages

Benefits of Waterfall

1

Clear Structure and Process Control

Each phase has defined inputs, outputs, and approval steps.

2

Strong Documentation

Detailed documentation makes knowledge transfer and compliance easier.

3

Easy to Measure Progress

Milestones make tracking project status straightforward.

4

Suitable for Stable Projects

Works well when requirements are unlikely to change.

5

Good for Regulated Environments

Ideal for industries requiring formal approvals and documentation (e.g., government, healthcare).

Waterfall Phases: Requirements → Design → Implementation → Testing → Deployment
Figure 2: Waterfall sequential phases

Disadvantages of Waterfall

1

Limited Flexibility

Changes are difficult and expensive once development begins.

2

Late Customer Feedback

Users often see the final product only near the end of the project.

3

Higher Risk of Requirement Errors

Mistakes in requirements may only appear during testing.

4

Not Ideal for Complex or Innovative Projects

When requirements evolve, Waterfall becomes inefficient.

Waterfall vs Agile: Quick Context

Choose Waterfall When:

  • Requirements are clear and stable
  • Regulatory compliance requires documentation
  • Customer wants fixed price and scope
  • Technology is well-understood

Choose Agile When:

  • Requirements will evolve
  • Customer feedback is needed early
  • Innovation and adaptability matter
  • Time-to-market is critical
