Chapter 7.3

Three Key Quality Processes

US-28 Quality Management

Planning, Assurance, and Control form a continuous cycle that protects project quality.

Why This Matters

Quality Management is not a single event that happens at the end of a project. Instead, it is a continuous cycle designed to ensure the project meets the requirements of the stakeholders. These activities are categorized into Planning, Assurance, and Control.

1. Quality Planning (The "Standard Setter")

Quality Planning is the process of identifying which quality standards are relevant to the project and determining how to satisfy them. It happens during the initiation and planning phases.

Objective: To prevent errors by defining what "Success" looks like.

Key Activities

Definition of Done (DoD)

Defining the Definition of Done (DoD).

Metrics

Setting Metrics (e.g., a maximum of 2 bugs per feature).

Standards

Identifying legal or industry standards (ISO, IEEE).

Analogy: If you are building a car, Quality Planning is the blueprint that specifies exactly what grade of steel must be used for the frame.

2. Quality Assurance / QA (The "Process Manager")

Quality Assurance focuses on the process used to create the deliverables. It is proactive and performed throughout the project's execution.

Objective: To provide confidence that the project will meet its quality standards by following the right procedures.

Quality Audits

  • Reviewing the team's workflow to see if they are following the agreed-upon steps.

Process Analysis

  • Identifying bottlenecks or "Quality Debt" in the production line.
Analogy: In car manufacturing, QA is the regular inspection of the assembly line machines to ensure they are calibrated correctly and the workers are following safety protocols.

3. Quality Control / QC (The "Product Checker")

Quality Control focuses on the deliverable itself. It is reactive and happens after a component or feature is built.

Objective: To identify specific defects in the final products before they reach the customer.

Peer Reviews

  • Having another developer check the code or text.

Testing

  • Running software tests or physical stress tests on a product.
Analogy: In car manufacturing, QC is the final test drive and the crash test performed on the finished car to ensure it actually works as promised.

Comparison Table for Students

Focus and Timing

  • Quality Planning: Standards and Metrics - before work begins.
  • Quality Assurance (QA): The Process - during the work.
  • Quality Control (QC): The Product - after work is done.

Nature and Goal

  • Quality Planning: Strategic - prevention.
  • Quality Assurance (QA): Proactive - process improvement.
  • Quality Control (QC): Reactive - defect identification.
