Project Quality Management

PMBOK & ISO 9000 Two Pillars of Quality
1.

The Clear Definition

Quality in project management is the degree to which a project's deliverables conform to agreed requirements and are fit for their intended use — while using resources efficiently and meeting stakeholder expectations.

Project Quality Management PMBOK® 6 | Project Quality Control | PMP® Training Videos | Edureka
2.

The Two Pillars of Quality

Every quality definition in project management rests on two classic principles (PMBOK, ISO 9000):

Pillar Meaning Plain-language question
Conformance to requirements You built exactly what you said you would build. "Did we do what we promised?"
Fitness for use The deliverable actually works for the end-user in their real environment. "Can the user actually use it?"

Example: A bridge that matches every engineering specification (conformance) but is too narrow for the traffic it serves (not fit for use) is poor quality.

3.

What "Good Quality" Means in Practice

Principle What it means for your project
Meets acceptance criteria Every requirement (stated or implied) is satisfied before you say "done."
Defects are within tolerance You have agreed thresholds (e.g., <2% rework, zero safety issues). Perfection is not required — predictable quality is.
Process is efficient You build quality in during the work (reviews, checks, tests) not just inspect at the end.
Stakeholder expectations are managed Quality does not mean "everything everyone wants." It means meeting what was agreed to.
No gold-plating Adding unrequested features or over-engineering reduces quality (wastes time, adds risk, delays value).
4.

What Quality Is NOT (Clearing Confusion)

Misconception Reality
Quality means expensive A cheap, simple chair that holds weight and does not break is high quality for its purpose. Cost does not define quality.
Quality means zero defects Zero defects may cost more than the value they deliver. Good quality means defects within agreed limits.
Quality is the same as grade Grade = number of features (basic vs. premium). Quality = how well those features work. Low grade + high quality is acceptable. High grade + low quality is failure.
Quality is only the tester's job Everyone owns quality. The PM plans it, the team builds it, the tester verifies it, the stakeholder confirms it.
Quality = perfection Perfection is not a project goal. "Good enough for stakeholder needs" is the target.
5.

Practical Examples by Project Type

Project Type Poor Quality Good Quality
Construction Walls are straight but electrical fails inspection. All specs met, signed off, client walkthrough passes.
Marketing campaign Sent to wrong audience, broken links, wrong branding. Segmented correctly, tested on devices, brand guide followed.
Event planning Venue booked but AV fails, catering is late. Run sheet followed, backup AV onsite, catering on time.
Manufacturing 10% of units fail within 1 week. Defect rate < 1%, meets safety and durability standards.
Internal report Data included but unverified, inconsistent format. Peer-reviewed, data sourced, template followed, approved.
Software development Works most of the time but crashes on edge cases. Works for all defined scenarios; edge cases handled.
6.

The One Rule of Thumb

Ask your key stakeholder: "If we deliver exactly this, will you be satisfied and able to use it?"

Answer Implication
Yes Quality is met.
"It works but I expected something else" Requirements gap — fix the requirements process, not the quality process.
"It is what I asked for but I cannot actually use it" Fitness for use missing — revisit user needs.
7.

Summary for Your Team

Good quality in project management means:

  • You delivered what was promised (conformance to requirements).
  • The user can actually use it (fitness for use).
  • Defects are within agreed limits (not perfection).
  • You did not waste time on unrequested extras (no gold-plating).
  • You caught issues early (prevention over inspection).

"Quality is not an act of inspection at the end. It is a habit of mind throughout the journey."
