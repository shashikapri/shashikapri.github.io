Chapter 6.4

The Captain's Log: Creating and Using a Risk Register

US-25 Risk Register

Document risks clearly, assign ownership, and track responses so your project can act early instead of reacting late.

User Story (US-25)

As a project manager/Scrum master, I want clear instructions on how to create a risk register, so that I can document and track possible risks in my project effectively.

What Is a Risk Register?

A risk register is a living document that lists possible risks, their likelihood and impact, who owns each risk, and what actions should be taken.

Think of it as your project's navigation log: it keeps the team aligned, improves transparency, and makes risk discussions practical in sprint planning and status reviews.

Use one shared register for the whole project to avoid duplicate or conflicting risk information.

Essential Fields to Include

Risk ID

Unique code (for example, R-001, R-002) to track and reference each risk.

Risk Description

Short statement of what might happen and how it could affect scope, time, cost, or quality.

Category

Technical, team, schedule, external, quality, or business risk.

Probability

How likely the risk is to happen (Low, Medium, High or 1-5 scale).

Impact

How serious the consequence is if the risk occurs (Low, Medium, High or 1-5 scale).

Risk Score

Priority score calculated with: Probability x Impact.

Owner

Person responsible for monitoring and driving risk response actions.

Response Plan

Chosen strategy: avoid, mitigate, transfer, or accept, with clear actions.

Status

Open, monitoring, escalated, or closed.

Review Date

Next check date so the team does not forget active risks.

Step-by-Step: Create Your Risk Register

1

Choose a format

Start with a simple table in Excel, Google Sheets, Notion, Jira, or your PM tool.

2

Brainstorm risks with the team

Use sprint planning, backlog refinement, and retrospectives to gather possible risks early.

3

Describe each risk clearly

Write in cause-effect format: "If [cause], then [risk event], resulting in [impact]."

4

Rate probability and impact

Use a shared scoring guide to keep ratings consistent between team members.

5

Assign an owner and response

Every high-priority risk must have one clear owner and one concrete action plan.

6

Review and update regularly

Update at least once per sprint and whenever major changes happen in scope or dependencies.

How to Prioritize Risks

Use a simple numeric model:

Risk Score = Probability x Impact

1-4: Low

Monitor during regular reviews.

5-9: Medium

Plan mitigation and track in each sprint.

10-25: High

Act immediately and escalate if needed.

ID Risk Category Prob. Impact Score Owner Response Status

R-003 Client feedback may be delayed before sprint review. External 2 3 6 Project Manager Book review slots in advance and send reminders 48h prior. Open

Using the Register During the Project

In Daily/Weekly Meetings

Review top 3 risks and confirm action progress.

In Sprint Planning

Add mitigation tasks into the sprint backlog where needed.

In Stakeholder Updates

Share major risks, current status, and decisions required.

In Retrospectives

Close outdated risks and capture new risks from recent lessons learned.
