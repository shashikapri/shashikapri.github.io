Chapter 2.3

Choosing Between Waterfall and Agile

US-6 Framework Selection

Selecting the right framework is a strategic decision. The choice depends on project characteristics, stakeholder expectations, and environmental factors.

Why This Matters for Scrum Masters

Understanding when to recommend Agile vs. Waterfall helps you guide stakeholders, set realistic expectations, and choose the right approach for each project context — avoiding framework mismatches that lead to failure.

Key Differences Between Waterfall and Agile

Figure 1: Waterfall vs. Agile — Key Differences at a Glance

WATERFALL

Process→ Sequential
Change→ Restricted
Documentation→ Heavy
Customer Role→ Start & End
Testing→ After Development
Best For:
  • Stable Projects
  • Clear Requirements

AGILE

Process→ Iterative
Change→ Welcomed
Documentation→ Light
Customer Role→ Continuous
Testing→ Ongoing
Best For:
  • Dynamic Projects
  • Evolving Requirements

Choose Waterfall When:

  • Requirements are clearly defined and stable
  • Scope, timeline, and budget are fixed
  • Strong documentation is required
  • The project operates in a regulated environment
  • The project risk is low and predictable

Choose Agile When:

  • Requirements may change frequently
  • Continuous customer feedback is required
  • The project involves innovation or uncertainty
  • Early delivery of partial functionality is valuable
  • Adaptability is critical to success

Decision-Making Framework for Project Managers

Figure 2: Waterfall vs. Agile Decision Model

This decision tree shows that requirement stability is a key factor in selecting a framework. Stable requirements favor Waterfall, while evolving requirements favor Agile.

Before selecting a framework, ask:

  1. How stable are the requirements?
  2. How often will changes occur?
  3. How much stakeholder involvement is needed?
  4. Are there regulatory constraints?
  5. How experienced is the team with Agile methods?

Careful evaluation of these factors helps project managers and Scrum Masters select the most appropriate approach and increase project success.

Key Insight: There is no "one-size-fits-all" framework. The best approach depends on your project's stability, complexity, and stakeholder needs. Use the decision model above to guide your choice.
