Change Management

Structured Governance Scrum & Agile Alignment

Definition (Guide-Book Version)

Change management is the structured, documented process used to capture, assess, approve, and implement any modification to a project's agreed baseline — whether that baseline relates to requirements, design, code, tests, schedule, or budget.
Association for Project Management (APM): “Change control is the process through which all requests to change the approved baseline of a project, programme or portfolio are captured, evaluated and then approved, rejected or deferred.”
In Agile environments such as Scrum, change is expected. However, unmanaged change introduces risk, confusion, and rework. Change management does not mean "no changes"; it means no surprise changes.
🔍 Key Insight: "One uncontrolled change can destabilize an entire project." — Disciplined change management protects delivery timelines, quality, and team focus.

What Change Management Includes (Clear & Actionable)

📋 Capturing every change request in a formal log or tool
📊 Assessing impact on scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources & risk
✅ Deciding whether to approve, reject, or defer the change
🔧 Implementing approved changes in a controlled, traceable manner
📄 Documenting decisions to maintain transparency & auditability

These steps ensure that the project team and stakeholders share a single, consistent understanding of what is changing and why.

What Change Management IS NOT

  • Not a mechanism to block all changes
  • Not unnecessary bureaucracy
  • Not limited to large or traditional projects
  • Not a replacement for Agile adaptability
  • Instead, it is a governance safeguard that ensures changes support — not sabotage — the project's objectives.

Why It Matters for Students & New Scrum Masters

  • Prevents scope creep
  • Protects sprint commitments
  • Ensures stakeholder alignment
  • Reduces rework and hidden costs
  • Supports predictable delivery
Understanding change management is essential for anyone responsible for project stability, stakeholder communication, and delivery outcomes.
